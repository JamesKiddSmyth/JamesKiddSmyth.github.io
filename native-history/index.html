Native Nations Through Time

    About this map

    Prototype. From 800 to 1600, rough cultural spheres known from archaeology (not nations' borders) and mound centres in the Southeast and Midwest. From 1600 to 1783 only the lower Great Lakes and the Ohio country are mapped (grey elsewhere). After 1783: United States treaty cessions and Canadian treaty areas. The drawn areas, the Mississippian spheres and the grouping of treaty names into nations are first drafts, not yet reviewed.

    Coloured areas are nations' lands (before 1600, archaeological cultures) as the cited sources describe them. Solid edges are documented boundaries (treaty maps); dashed edges are estimates, drawn for this project from published descriptions. Click an area or a marker for dates, accounts, sources and links.

    Where accounts disagree, each is given with whose account it is, and every proposed site is marked. Haudenosaunee oral accounts are not yet included for most events.

    Blank land is not empty land: it may have been shared hunting ground, or simply not yet estimated. Native people stayed on or returned to much ceded land.

    Point at the map to outline every area under the pointer; where areas overlap, a note names them. The address bar keeps the year and view, so the page can be shared or bookmarked as it is.

    Boundaries shown are scholarly estimates. Many territories were shared, seasonal or overlapping, and nations' own histories may differ from these sources.
